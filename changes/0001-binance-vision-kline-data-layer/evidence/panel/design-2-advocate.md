## Verdict
Do not accept the deferral as written: close the concern at design with a reasoned decision on layout and codec level, and turn the build-time "measurement" into either reproducible evidence or an owner-run check after merge.

## The case against
The obvious resolution ("keep one file per day, zstd 3, measure in the build, the owner decides later") answers the owner's question with a ritual, not a decision. The intent's reason for the question is on its first page: "the owner's disk space is limited, so the cache has to stay small". The design then picks, unmeasured, the layout with the most per-file overhead (365 Parquet footers per symbol-interval-year) and the lowest useful zstd level for a write-once, read-many cache, where a higher level costs one slower write per day and nothing on read.
The build measurement cannot change anything: cache.py, `clear_cache`'s name pattern, closed-day REST caching and the atomic write are all built around per-day files first (plan steps 7-8), the measurement comes at step 10, and the spec itself says "switching layout would be a later change". A measurement that cannot alter the thing it measures is not the measurement the intent asked for.
It also measures the wrong thing and leaves no proof. Synthetic random-walk floats do not compress like real BTCUSDT 1m (tick-size prices, repeated values, round volumes), so the figure answers neither "is per-day small enough" nor "which level". And plan step 10 generates the data in a throwaway script, outside the repository, committed nowhere: `evidence/storage-measurement.md` will be two numbers the writer typed, which no verifier, reviewer or future reader can reproduce. The spec's own Acceptance makes this unverifiable claim a pass criterion.
Finally, the real measurement is impossible in any phase (no Binance host in the sandbox or CI, ever), so "carried forward to the build" is carried forward to nowhere; the only machine that can measure real data is the owner's.
What I would decide instead: close the concern now with an estimate the design can stand on (1440 rows x 11 columns per 1m day is tens of KB after zstd; Parquet's per-file metadata is one or two KB, so per-day overhead is a few percent, acceptable against the atomicity and closed-day-caching benefits), pick the level on the write-once argument and state it, drop the synthetic figure from Acceptance, and give the owner the two commands that measure real data after merge (`download BTCUSDT 1m <year>` then `cache-info`) with the threshold that would reopen the layout (say, more than 20% over a monthly file).
I would accept the obvious resolution if the owner says a synthetic figure is enough for them, or if the generator script is committed under `tests/` or `evidence/` so the figures are reproducible and the build run's verifier can re-run it.
