## Verdict
Refuse `frame.height <= slow` (not `<`) at design, and pin both sides of the boundary in the proof; the literal `<` is an off-by-one against the intent's own stated reason.

## The case against
1. The intent gives the rule and its reason in one clause: "fewer candles than `--slow`, so the strategy could never take a position". The two disagree on exactly one height. At `height == slow` the slow SMA exists on the last row only (`rolling_mean` with `min_samples` = window, plan step 1), so the only possible `1` is `positions[last]`, which `_simulate` never reads (`app/backtest/engine.py` lines 63–64 iterate `positions[row - 1]` for `row in 1..len-1`; change 0004 R8). The strategy "could never take a position" there; the reason refuses it, the number admits it. The reason is the requirement; the number is its mis-transcription.
2. Keeping `<` makes the command print a result that is not one: `trades 0`, `total return 0.00%`, `time in market 0.00%` on a range the owner's own words call unusable. ux-and-brand rule 3 ("error and empty states are designed, not left to defaults") is sidestepped: the empty state is dressed as a success with exit 0, and the person is told nothing about what to do next (rule 4), which the `error:` message already does ("widen the dates or lower --slow").
3. `<=` is the exact set: at `height == slow + 1` the slow SMA exists on rows `slow-1` and `slow`, a `1` on row `slow-1` fills at `open[slow]` and closes at the last close (0004 R9), one trade. Nothing legitimate is lost: the positions are never shown (trade list is out of scope), so "see whether the signal fires on the last candle" is not a use the CLI serves.
4. The plan's proof hides the choice either way: `test_run_refuses_too_few_candles` uses `--slow 30` on 24 candles, which passes under `<` and `<=` alike. The boundary case (`--slow 24` on 24 candles) is untested, so "one comparison and its test change" later would be changing an untested line; the design should pin `--slow 24` → exit 2 and `--slow 23` → exit 0 now.
5. The design's message, "the range has 30 candles, fewer than slow 50", becomes false on the equal case under `<=`; wording it "the range has N candles, not more than slow S: the strategy could never take a position" costs nothing and keeps the intent's reason in the user's hands.

What I would decide: spec R12 and Design step (5) say `frame.height <= slow`, with the message above, and the proof adds the two boundary cases. What would make me accept `<` after all: the owner saying in so many words that the number, not the reason, is the rule (a deliberate "show me the warm-up-only run"), plus a test that pins `height == slow` to exit 0 with `trades 0`, so the behaviour is chosen rather than inherited from an off-by-one.
